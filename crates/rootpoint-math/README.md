# rootpoint-math

The math of rootpoint: a formula as a tree, its closed subset of LaTeX, the table of LaTeX commands,
and the layout of a formula from an OpenType math font into boxes and glyphs. It depends on no other
crate of the workspace, reads no file, and builds for the browser.

Its public API:

- `Formula` is a grid of rows and cells, each cell a list of `Atom`s: a symbol, a fraction, a
  radical, scripts, a `\left…\right` group, an accent, an operator name such as `\sin`, a font
  change, a `\text` run, a matrix, a space, a group, or an unknown command kept by its name.
  `Formula::parse` reads LaTeX of the subset and refuses what it cannot read: an unbalanced brace,
  a second superscript or subscript on one nucleus, an `\end` with no `\begin`, a missing argument,
  an environment it does not know, a nesting deeper than `MAX_DEPTH`.
  `Formula::to_latex` writes it back; a formula that keeps the invariants `Formula` states, as
  every formula `parse` gives does, is written as a text that reads back equal. Atoms nest at most
  `MAX_DEPTH` deep.
- `commands` and `command_of` read the table of commands: each LaTeX name with its character and
  its `Class`, only the characters of the built-in math font, generated by `cargo x math-commands`
  from W3C `unicode.xml`, whose notice is `W3C-NOTICE.txt`. `command` reads a name of the table,
  or of a character the font lacks, which a formula may hold and which is drawn as the font's
  missing glyph.
- `MathFont` holds a font file's bytes with a MATH table; `lay_out` lays a formula out with it, in
  display or text `Style`, at a size, and gives a `FormulaLayout`: the glyphs and rules to draw,
  and a box per atom and per cell, each with its `CellPath`, so an editor places a caret on what
  is drawn. Every value is in the units of the size, and a layout at `k` times a size is the layout
  at the size scaled by `k`. An empty cell is a placeholder half an em wide; an empty group or
  nucleus takes no width, as in TeX.

The caller draws the layout: rootpoint-render does it with the built-in Libertinus Math.
